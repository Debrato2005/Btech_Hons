D. C. Motors
A four-pole d.c. motor 
• As the armature rotates, the commutator reverses the 
current in each coil as it moves between the field poles.
• This is necessary if the forces acting on the coil are to remain 
acting in the same direction and if the rotation is to continue. 
• In the conventional d.c. motor, coils of wire are mounted in slots on a cylinder of 
magnetic material called the armature.
•
The direction of rotation can be reversed either by 
      reversing the armature current or the field current.
